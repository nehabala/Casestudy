OverviewSustainability and Design
Introduction

A module designed to shift perspective

This module helps Class 10 students understand why sustainable design is urgently needed — and builds their capacity to act on that understanding through hands-on learning.

  • —Helps students learn why we need to design sustainably, and some ways to assess and do so
  • —Attempts to bring about an attitude shift — making students more conscious of their decisions as future creators

Boundaries

What is in scope?

  • —Sustainable design through the lens of those involved in the design of products
  • —Focuses on products only — not services or systems
  • —Designed for a physical classroom setting
  • —Assumes availability of all required resources and equally competent teachers

Design PhaseSustainability and Design
Pedagogical Approach

Building on proven learning theory

Constructionist Theories

Knowledge is built on existing knowledge through active engagement. Students learn by doing — creating artefacts that make understanding visible and tangible.

  • —Learning by doing
  • —Building on prior knowledge
  • —Active engagement in the process

Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle

Learning progresses through four stages: concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation.

The module's activities walk students through all four stages for every major concept — ensuring depth and durability of learning.

Design Phase13 / 26
Design Phase · ActivitiesSustainability and Design
Class Activities

Experiencing the concepts

Activity 01 · Skit

Understanding human impact

Students enact how people from different historical eras obtained their lunch — hunter-gatherer to industrial. Getting into the details of each era makes the growing environmental cost of human progress visceral and memorable.

Activity 02 · Ecosystem Mapping

Mrs. Preetha's pickles

Students listen to a story about Mrs. Preetha and her town, then individually draw the ecosystem of her pickle-making process before collaborating to deepen and critique each other's maps.

Design Phase · Activities14 / 26
Design Phase · ActivitiesSustainability and Design
Class Activities

Making it stick

Activity 03 · Impromptu Skit

Lifecycle thinking in action

Students act out the phases of Mrs. Preetha's pickle lifecycle. Embodying each stage reveals process gaps and inefficiencies that brainstorming alone tends to miss.

Activity 04 · Charades

Sustainability strategies through play

Students find creative ways to explain sustainability strategies non-verbally to classmates. Teaching others is among the most effective ways to consolidate understanding.

Design Phase · Long ActivitySustainability and Design
Long Activity · Groups of 3

Redesigning a product across five stages

Students choose a product and progressively apply each session's learnings to analyse and redesign it. Class time is allocated throughout the module.

Session 1
Choose a product
Groups select the product they'll work with for the entire module.
Session 2
Map the ecosystem
Understand the present ecosystem, all stakeholders, and current issues.
Session 3
Analyse the lifecycle
Trace the full lifecycle and identify where resources and value are lost.
Sessions 4–5
Redesign
Redesign product and lifecycle using sustainability strategies. Map new ecosystem.
Session 6
Present & discuss
Group presents the redesign. Q&A session with peers and teacher.

Evaluation

Three modes of assessment

Session-wise Performance

Activity scores averaged within each topic → topic scores averaged per session → final score averaged across all five sessions.

Long Activity Performance

Rubric-based evaluation at the end of sessions 2, 3, 5, and 6. Covers ecosystem mapping, lifecycle analysis, redesign quality, and final presentation.

Self-Assessment

Students reflect on their own change in perspective — measured against the desired attitude shift objectives. Completed at the start and end of the module to track the shift.

Methodology

Feedback from science and EVS teachers

Initial Evaluation Rounds

Teachers at CBSE schools in Chennai and Mumbai consulted at each project stage. Key feedback:

  • —Need to assess individual contributions within group activities
  • —Evaluation plan is difficult to picture and interpret
  • —Need for structured reflection after each activity
  • —"Umbrella questions" help both teacher and student track progress

Final Evaluation Plan

Scheduled for May · 3-week process
  • —Week 1 — Contact 5–6 teachers, cognitive walkthroughs
  • —Week 2 — Document all feedback and suggestions
  • —Week 3 — Collate feedback, identify weaknesses, propose solutions
